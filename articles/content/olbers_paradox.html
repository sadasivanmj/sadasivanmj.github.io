Why is Night dark?

A reflection on darkness, fear, and the cosmological reasons the night sky remains black.

“I have loved the stars too fondly to be fearful of the night.”

These are the verses from the poem “Old astronomer” of one of the significant poets who belonged to the Victorian era, Sarah Williams. This particular poem is taken from the collection Twilight hours: A legacy of verse. The poem is written from the perspective of an aged astronomer on his deathbed bidding his student to continue his humble research.

Most of our life happens at daytime, when sun gives way to every path we take, every chore we do. Back when the world was not lit up by neon lights and tall illuminating buildings, Night was an uncertain hour. It was because of how much we could not see. Lurking predators, poisoned reptiles, etc.…

Moreover, it was the fear of something we could not see, seeing us. Danger is a lot easier to fight if it can be detected. And Night did not allow for much detection of it. Evolution programmed brain to watch the darkness as less safe.

Still, amidst the biological causes, some find darkness their close companions. It can be explained using the same logic but in reverse. When the world progressed and Night became astoundingly close to how days were treated, humans were not required to “survive” the dark anymore. When survival became easy on the outside, tides began turning inside.

The social structure humans created to maintain their lives began to tire them. Day time belonged to that social structure or society. To work, to perform, to entertain. But nights belonged to us. If creativity, intellect and hard work was for the world then we had calm, peace and tranquility. Night does not demand us to be seen.

We could simply exist with our flaws and darkness won’t betray by revealing it to the world.

The night sky offers something much grander than it’s darkness. The stars. We look up and see stars here and there and much over there stretched across the black canvas. If stars are supposed to emit light then the sky should be illuminating with blazing stars. But why not? this contradiction even has a name. It is called “Olbers’ paradox”

‘If the universe were static, homogeneous at a large scale, and populated by an infinite number of stars, any line of sight from Earth must end at the surface of a star and if light from an infinite distance could reach Earth, the night sky should be completely illuminated and very bright.’

This was the historical argument in astrophysics which led to the birth of this paradox. Before modern cosmology emerged, experts and wonderers made several explanations. Some of them go like this:

  • Dust absorbing the light
  • Uneven distribution of stars

Both these explanations are flawed as on absorbing the radiation, the dust itself could get heated up and glow re-emitting the light. Even if stars are unevenly distributed over infinite distances, the light would still accumulate. Clumping changes where stars are not how much light exists.

The foundation of this paradox was the assumption that Universe is static and eternal. What if it is not?

Universe is not eternal

Universe is 13.8 billion years old. Which implies light haven’t gotten enough time to cover all the spaces. There are parts of cosmos whose light has not reached us yet. The darkness we see is not the absence of light but simply time preventing the light of those to fall upon us.

In a way, we are looking at the past of our universe at the same time we are living our present.

The boundary of our visibility is not where reality ends. It is where reality stops being perceived by the observer.

Universe is not static

Space expands. Light’s wavelength also gets stretched as it travels through it. which is known as “Cosmological redshift” If the light comes from a long way, it could shift into infrared, microwave or radio wavelengths making it invisible to us.

The universe does shine not just in the way we can capture it but in the form of Cosmic microwave background which is the cooled afterglow of big-bang.

Universe is dark because light has been travelling so long that it no longer contains its own self.

Next time your eyes get nailed to those dark spots between each burning star, remember it is not just void. Either we are in the early chapters and photons are still travelling to tell us their story or that their stories got scattered, transformed into frequencies that we cannot yet understand.

Remember that the present and past of universe coexist within the observer and know that across time there exists a future which might become past for those who get to see it.
